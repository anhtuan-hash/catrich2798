ENGLISH 11 · GLOBAL SUCCESS · UNIT 5

Our Climate Action Museum

From real group-priority surveys to sourced English exhibits and documented revisions.

2 periods · 45 + 45 minutes · 11 activities

A read-only guide for teachers and reviewers. No account required; no access to student drafts, accounts or personal data.

Open Museum Lab
This page contains the lesson plan and teaching materials. Authentic classroom evidence is exported separately from the teacher account.

Objectives and learning products

Write three Problem–Evidence–Solution exhibit captions (about 25–40 words each); report authentic data with N and limitations; create original artwork; exchange peer feedback; document one revision and its reason; and present a 45–60-second English pitch.

90-minute lesson sequence

1. Climate Detective Period 1 · 5 min

Warm-up and problem framing

Which everyday school action could best reduce our environmental impact?

I think … because …

Teacher: Show two contrasting school situations. Ask students to predict which action is feasible and give one English reason.

Products and assessment: A feasible action must match the school situation. This is a warm-up vote, NOT research evidence.

2. Language Mini-Lesson Period 1 · 10 min

Language input and expression

Practise evidence, cause and recommendation language.

According to …, … . Therefore, we recommend … .

Teacher: Model: According to… / This may lead to… / We recommend… Students answer three quick questions in groups.

Products and assessment: Answers: 1A, 2B, 3B. Reveal only after teacher clicks Show Explanation.

3. Live Classroom Survey Period 1 · 10 min

Live classroom survey

As a group, choose ONE action your group will prioritise at school.

In our group-priority survey, … out of N groups selected … . This does not measure individual habits.

Teacher: Open the survey. Each group discusses and chooses ONE priority. N counts groups, never individual habits. Freeze the survey before interpreting or pinning its chart.

Products and assessment: Use real N and percentages. Do NOT generalise a class sample to the whole school.

4. Problem · Evidence · Solution Period 1 · 12 min

Analyze and design a solution

How can our school reduce one environmental problem?

One problem is … . Evidence from … suggests … . We recommend … because … .

Teacher: Assign roles: English editor, data checker, artwork designer and presenter. Ask for a verifiable problem, a cited observation, and one feasible response.

Products and assessment: Check source, English accuracy and feasibility.

5. Storyboard Checkpoint Period 1 · 8 min

Plan three exhibits

Plan a three-exhibit museum: Problem, Evidence, Solution.

Our first exhibit shows … . Our second exhibit uses … .

Teacher: Demonstrate the three cards and the success checklist. Have groups draw a poster plan and prepare captions.

Products and assessment: At least three planned labels and their sources; no invented figures.

6. Recap and Resume Period 2 · 4 min

Recap and reopen drafts

What makes an English museum exhibit reliable and persuasive?

Our exhibits must be clear, sourced and useful.

Teacher: Review source, English readability, feasibility and visitor experience. Unlock artwork production next.

Products and assessment: Have learners reopen the previous version of their draft.

7. Create the 3D Museum Period 2 · 18 min

Upload artwork and build the 3D museum

Upload an original poster, caption it in English, and see it framed on the museum wall.

This artwork illustrates … . We chose … because … .

Teacher: Demonstrate one genuine image upload. Students photograph or export their own poster/infographic, add captions (25–40 English words suggested), and choose one of three rooms.

Products and assessment: At least one actual artwork with a source label, real 3D placement and accessible 2D fallback.

8. Peer Gallery Walk Period 2 · 8 min

Peer gallery walk and feedback

What did you learn? What could make this exhibit clearer?

I learned … . The message would be clearer if … .

Teacher: Open a temporary class-only peer preview. Require constructive English feedback; do not publicly publish drafts.

Products and assessment: Use real visitor feedback, not fabricated claims.

9. Improve the Exhibition Period 2 · 6 min

Improve after testing

Revise one caption or poster based on feedback.

We changed … because our visitors said … .

Teacher: Compare before/after and ask teams to state why their change improves communication.

Products and assessment: Preserve authentic revision evidence.

10. Present and Discuss Period 2 · 6 min

Present and discuss

Explain your problem, one authentic figure with N, and one practical solution.

According to our group survey (N=…), … . We propose … .

Teacher: Spotlight two or three group museums for 45–60-second pitches. Invite one peer question.

Products and assessment: Teacher can tour a private group draft without giving it public visibility.

11. Exit Ticket Period 2 · 3 min

Reflect and apply

What environmental action will you take, and why?

I will … because … .

Teacher: Summarise the English communication skills and release the exit ticket. Groups then submit their frozen museum draft.

Products and assessment: This is a group/device exit ticket. TTCM approval happens after the lesson.

Learning materials and tasks

1 · Understand the problem

Burning fossil fuels adds greenhouse gases to the atmosphere. These gases trap heat and contribute to global warming. A school action can address one part of the problem, but a classroom vote cannot measure how much warming that action prevents.

Underline a cause and its result. Explain the connection using because.

NASA · What Is Climate Change?

2 · Compare feasible actions

Walking or cycling where safe, switching off unused lights and reducing food waste are possible climate actions. Choose an action that fits your school. Explain who will do it, what resources it needs and one practical limitation.

Recommend one action. Explain why it is feasible and identify a limitation.

United Nations · Ten Actions

3 · Read evidence carefully

Our survey records one priority choice per group. N is the number of groups that voted. Report the count, N, percentage and date together. These choices do not show individual habits, represent the whole school or measure emissions saved.

Complete: … out of … groups selected … . We cannot conclude that … .

BRIAN · Original evidence-literacy activity

4 · Write for a visitor

A clear exhibit states a problem, gives relevant evidence and recommends a practical response. Use according to for a source, because for a reason and therefore for a result. Write 25–40 words per exhibit, then revise after a visitor tests the message.

Write three captions. Ask a peer what they learned and what needs clarification.

Conclusion and application

A persuasive English exhibit links a clear problem, a source and a feasible recommendation. Group priorities describe our class discussion, not individual behaviour or measured emission reductions. Transfer: choose one feasible action, state who will carry it out and explain how you would check it next week.

Paper-based contingency

Before class, download the lesson and resource cards. If the network fails, use printed group ballots, paper storyboards and peer slips (learned / clarify / clarity 1–4). Record group labels and time; keep the original papers. Enter the paper tally only if no online survey votes exist. Upload authentic work when the network returns. The online classroom does not synchronise offline edits automatically.

How to preserve learning evidence

  1. Freeze the Stage 3 survey; record one choice per group. Never combine online votes with paper ballots.
  2. Wait for the Saved indicator before switching tasks. The system checkpoints the storyboard after Stage 5, the pre-feedback version after Stage 7, and the revised version after Stage 9.
  3. Attach the frozen chart to the Evidence exhibit in Artwork Studio. Groups must write their own interpretation.
  4. Peer feedback is round-robin: each group submits and receives one response.
  5. Export P1/P2 and evidence files from Competition Dossier. Check sharing permissions and keep the final P2 package within 50 MB.
